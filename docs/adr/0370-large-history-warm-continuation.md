# ADR-0370. Prefer a warm Worker for large settled Session histories

Accepted for the isolated #52 composition. Use the existing directed wake socket, normal PG claims and disposable Worker-local cache. The earlier [continuation experiment](../reports/session-continuation-throughput-20261119.md) and its [Temporal sticky execution comparison](../reports/worker-claim-sizing-20261119.md) support bounded affinity with cold fallback.

Each normal completion may offer immutable native Entries, including image blocks, under the tenant, logical Session, writable Flow and terminal fact ID. Only a PG-confirmed predecessor of the newly leased Turn may consume that snapshot. Fresh model, tools, workspace, mailbox and ownership always come from the new Turn. A change of Flow or version, pruning, expiry, process loss, or an active handoff selects the cold path. The cache has a byte budget and an idle expiry and occupies no execution slots.

For expensive histories, a connected but busy cache holder gets a short first opportunity. PG's existing ready-row timestamp bounds deferral even if every wake is lost. An exact Turn hint can claim immediately; unrelated FIFO work remains eligible. Lost and stale hints never grant ownership. The scheduling hint carries identifiers, a version and timing metadata. Tune waiting by measuring both PG bytes and admission-to-model time. The serving cutover remains gated under #48.

The candidate defaults to 256 MiB of serialized Entry payloads per Worker, a 256 KiB admission minimum and five-minute retention. The 16 MiB scheduling threshold and two-second grace are candidate defaults. The PG ready trigger consults the predecessor’s compact history head. An inherited range can also qualify for the grace; on a live router only a matching cache advertisement of sufficient size can hold back an available alternative. FIFO claims after the deadline work even after losing the entire router. Fork windows start cold and subsequently reuse their own confirmed prefixes. Detailed [acceptance and limits](../reports/warm-image-continuation-20261127.md).
